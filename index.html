My Land, My Plan

My Land, My Plan helps a farmer turn basic details about an underused plot into practical options and clear next steps for checking local support.

No account needed. Your details stay on this device only.

What you enter

District, village, plot size, water access, terrain, and the main constraint you face. Soil test is optional.

What you get

Two or three activity options to explore, a typical year for each, a comparison, a next-step checklist, and the official portals to check.

What this is not

This is a working prototype, not a government service. The options it suggests are illustrative. Always confirm crop choices, costs, and current scheme rules with your local agriculture office or Kisan Khidmat Ghar.

      About, sources and limitations

      My Land, My Plan is a prototype, not an official government service of the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir or any department.

      My Land, My Plan helps a farmer turn basic details about an underused plot into practical options and clear next steps for checking local support.

      What this app does

      • Collects basic, non-identifying plot details you choose to enter
      • Shows illustrative activity options using simple sample logic, with the reasons it picked them
      • Describes a typical year for each option, as general guidance
      • Shows general weather for a nearby town from Open-Meteo, when you are online
      • Links to official portals, without inventing amounts, deadlines or eligibility rules
      • Saves a draft on this device and can print, copy or download the plan

      What this app does not do

      • Does not verify land ownership or land records
      • Does not assess your particular plot, predict yield, or guarantee that a crop will suit it
      • Does not approve subsidies, decide eligibility, or list documents you would need
      • Does not give a field-level weather forecast, frost warning or irrigation schedule
      • Does not collect personal, financial, or land-identification data beyond what you type for this plan

      What is yours, what is illustrative, what is live

      • Your answers. The district, size, water, terrain and constraint you typed. These drive the plan.
      • Illustrative. The option list, the five ratings, the reasons shown for each suggestion, the calendars and the checklist. These come from simple rules and published general information. They are not an assessment.
      • Regional background. The district grouping, elevation bands and crop patterns, taken from the published sources below. These describe an area, never your field.
      • Live weather. Fetched from Open-Meteo when you ask for it, for a named town. It changes, and it is the only live data in the app.

      Sources

      Regional background comes from these published sources. Nothing about crops, zones or programmes was invented for this app.

      • Government of India, Department of Agriculture and Cooperation, Farmer's Guide for Jammu and Kashmir

        Used for the three agro-climatic zones, the elevation and rainfall gradient across the region, the leading crops of the Kashmir and Jammu zones, and the share of India's temperate fruit grown here.

      • ICAR, Jammu and Kashmir

        Used for the share of cultivated area, the rainfed proportion, the horticulture based farming systems of the valley, and the usual cropping systems in both regions.

      • CSIR Indian Institute of Integrative Medicine, saffron production

        Used for the fact that saffron is grown mainly in Pulwama, Budgam, Srinagar and Kishtwar.

      • Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana, National Mission on Saffron

        Used for the place of the Pampore belt in Pulwama within saffron production, and the place of the karewa uplands.

      • NITI Aayog, Roadmap for Horticulture Development in the UT of Jammu and Kashmir

        Used for the broad agro-climatic zone structure of the Union Territory and the districts that fall in the subtropical and intermediate zones.

      • Open-Meteo

      Limitations

      • The district grouping into three broad zones is coarse. Every district in J&K spans more than one zone, and a single village can hold a range of soil and elevation.
      • The elevation bands are approximate ranges, not survey figures.
      • The option ratings and the reasons shown for each suggestion come from a small set of hand-written rules, not from any agronomic model, soil data or yield data.
      • The calendars describe usual windows. Sowing and harvest move with elevation, variety and the year.
      • Saffron is only offered for the four districts where it is documented as grown, and the app cannot tell whether a particular piece of ground is suitable.
      • No conversions between kanal, acre, hectare and marla are exact, and local usage varies.
      • The text is in English only. Real use would need Kashmiri, Dogri, Urdu and Hindi, checked by fluent speakers.
      • Nothing here has been reviewed by an agriculture officer, a horticulturist or a legal expert.

      Data and privacy

      Draft plans are saved in your browser's local storage, on this device only. Nothing is uploaded, and there are no accounts, analytics or tracking. The weather screen sends only the district name you select to Open-Meteo. Use Start over inside the form, or clear your browser data, to remove a saved draft.

      Before real-world use

      Every option, rating and calendar here would need review by the district agriculture or horticulture office before a farmer acted on it. The scheme list would need to be replaced with the current official position for each district, checked at the time it is shown. The weather screen would need a proper location picker rather than a district name match.
